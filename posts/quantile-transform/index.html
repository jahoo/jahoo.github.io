Through the quantile function

A uniform carried through the inverse CDF has the law of X

2026-10-07 · Jacob Hoover Vigly · unlisted · exploration

Let \(U\) be uniform on \([0, 1]\). Then \(F_X^{-1}(U)\) has the law of \(X\), which is why sampling by the inverse CDF works. Evenly spaced values of \(u\) land at \(x = F_X^{-1}(u)\) with spacing \(\dee u / p_X(x)\), so they bunch where \(p_X\) is high: the density is the reciprocal of the quantile function’s slope. This builds on an earlier note on the density of a transformed random variable; here the transformation is the one that produces any law from a uniform.
